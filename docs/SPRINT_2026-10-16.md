## 3. Market and ecosystem research

**Method:** primary sources first (Coinbase CDP docs, x402 Foundation GitHub, AWS, vendor sites).

### 3.1 Established facts

**x402 governance and scale**
- **x402 Foundation (Linux Foundation).** About 40 members at launch, including AWS, Cloudflare, Coinbase, Google, Mastercard, Shopify, Stripe, Visa and the Solana Foundation. Dates differ by source: launch Apr 2, 2026, "operational" Jul 14, 2026.
  - [CDP: x402 Foundation](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/support/x402-foundation) †
  - [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4198170/new-linux-foundation-project-aims-to-make-payments-native-to-ai-workflows)
  - [TFTC](https://www.tftc.io/x402-foundation-operational-launch-ai-agent-payments)
- The spec repo now lives at `x402-foundation/x402`.

**CDP Bazaar (discovery)**
- Public catalogue, no auth, over REST, SDK search (`searchX402Resources`, top 20) and a hosted MCP server.
- Indexing is triggered by a payment through the CDP facilitator, and requires a 402 answer to the crawler.
- **Each result has a `quality` field: 30-day call count, unique payers, last called.** Ranking blends relevance with quality. Endpoints without traffic sink, then drop out.
- Coinbase itself calls it early ("Yahoo search").
  - [CDP: Discover services](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/bazaar) †
  - [CDP FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/support/faq) †
  - [x402 docs: Bazaar](https://docs.x402.org/extensions/bazaar)
- Paid services that settled are sometimes not indexed (a user report, Jul 2026): [coinbase/cdp-sdk#764](https://github.com/coinbase/cdp-sdk/issues/764).

**Bazaar demand is extremely concentrated** (community crawls, Oct 2026)†:
- Median of 1 payer and 2 calls; the top 10 listings take about 80% of calls.
- About 65% of listings have exactly one paying wallet (probably the seller's own test); only 1.1% have 10 or more.
- Chain-read and RPC endpoints draw the most payers.
- Bazaar calls are about 4% of facilitator transactions.
- Sources:
  - [lowkeybuilds crawl (13 Oct snapshot)](https://dev.to/lowkeybuilds/i-crawled-all-31620-x402-bazaar-listings-most-have-one-payer-and-boring-chain-reads-win-5c73)

**Real reliability failures, documented in the protocol's own issue tracker** (primary sources):
- **Settled, but the client sees a failure:**
  - Next.js turns settled 204/205/304 responses into a 402 and drops the receipt ([x402#3732](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/3732)).
  - Facilitator timeout while the funds moved ([x402#2294](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/2294)).
  - The Python MCP client drops settlement metadata ([x402#3149](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/3149)).
- **Paid, but the handler failed:** the server settles before its handler runs, so a 500 arrives alongside a successful PAYMENT-RESPONSE ([x402#3680](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/3680)).
- **Failed settlement still delivered:** [datafund/swarm_connect#355](https://github.com/datafund/swarm_connect/issues/355).
- **Missing receipt, so settlement "unknown":** [run402#593](https://github.com/kychee-com/run402/issues/593).
- **Failed settlement not reported in PAYMENT-RESPONSE:** [obolus#78](https://github.com/geekinasuit/obolus/issues/78).

### 3.3 Market hypotheses (to test in this sprint, not facts)

- **H1, the gap:** nobody publishes reproducible evidence that the *paid* response was delivered and valid (settlement receipt + delivery + schema, over time). x402 Trust says explicitly that it doesn't. The protocol's issue tracker shows settled-but-failed cases are real and confusing.
- **H2, who feels the pain:** the long tail (a median of 1 payer) has no revenue and won't pay for monitoring. The pain is felt by the **~1–5% of sellers with real paying traffic**, and by **platforms** that host or route many endpoints (Bankr x402 Cloud, marketplaces, AgentCore integrators). A broken paid response costs them money and reputation.
- **H3, what agents need before paying** (from competitors' verdict designs plus the failure modes above):
  1. Is it up now?
  2. Are its terms valid and its price as expected?
  3. When paid recently, did it deliver valid output?
  4. Latency.
  5. Did the price change?
  6. Does it have open incidents?

  All of this should come as a simple verdict, with evidence links. CORTX is the only one in the table above that has (3).
- **H4, Cori without noise:** rank by Bazaar demand (unique payers, excluding 1-payer listings) × our own liveness × company quality. That beats rule-only filtering, and it's free.
- **H5, willingness to pay:** providers may pay a small monthly fee (or credits) for a delivery-verified badge, incident alerts and evidence for disputes. Platforms may pay for bulk verification. Agents may pay per preflight call (x402-paid, ~$0.001–0.01). **No evidence yet:** zero customer conversations recorded.

### 3.4 Recommendations from research
1. **Position:** "delivery-verified evidence for x402", not "another trust score". Lead with the settled-but-not-delivered problem, which the x402 issue tracker itself documents.
2. **Use the Bazaar `quality` block in Cori** to find the few sellers with real buyers. This replaces the planned Basescan key.
3. **Target:** sellers with ≥ 10 unique payers (30-day), plus platforms (Bankr x402 Cloud). Don't target the long tail.
4. **Base-only is a known limit** (Solana is ~70% of volume). Note it; don't expand during this sprint.

## 9. Risks, spending controls and rollback

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Losing the history | S2 backup first; no destructive migrations; additive-only changes |
| Overspending | Caps (recommend $1/day, $10/month); sprint budget approved by the founder; per-check price cap; external services ≤ $0.01/check unless approved; spend shown by SQL F weekly |
| Draining the wallet via `/report` | Already limited ($0.25/day, ≤ $0.01 services, inside caps) |
| Key exposure | Key stays only in Vercel; never on the Cori server, never in chat; no new place gets it |
| Public false claims about third parties | Nothing published without reproduction + founder confirmation + provider consent (Cori brief) |
| Production schema drift | Every migration in numbered blocks with a check row; read `information_schema` first |
| Supabase free/nano limits | DB size each week (SQL G); Cori writes are mostly no-op for unchanged listings |
| Outreach seen as spam | Small targeted list, a founder-approved message each, one follow-up, honour "no" |
| Founder bandwidth (non-developer) | One step at a time; read-only first; each step says which window |

**Rollback:**
- **Vercel:** "Promote" the previous deployment (instant).
- **Cori:** `sudo /opt/cori/app/ops/cori/deploy.sh <previous-sha>`, or `sudo systemctl stop cori`. Cori holds no key, so stopping it is harmless.
- **Migrations:** additive only, so the old code keeps working; never drop columns in this sprint.
- **External provider monitoring:** pause the service. History is kept, never deleted.

## 10. Recommended first implementation task

**Before any code:** the Week 1 read-only measurement and the founder's S2 backup set-up. These protect the moat and turn the unknowns into numbers.

**First code task (after approval): T3, Cori demand ranking.**

Why this one:
- It directly feeds Week 2 (finding 15–20 providers with real buyers).
- It answers the founder's complaint that Cori's finds aren't "solid".
- It uses a free signal Coinbase already publishes.
- It spends nothing and adds nothing public.

Scope:
1. Read `quality` (30-day payers, calls, last called) from each Bazaar listing.
2. Store it append-only, at most once a day per service: a new `discovery_demand` table in migration 030, RESTRICT foreign keys, no updates.
3. Rank company cards and the Watching list by unique payers. 1-payer listings are labelled "no outside buyers yet".
4. Show payers and calls on each card.
5. Tests for the parser and the ranking, plus the real-Postgres test.

The first step of T3 is a read-only `curl` on the server to confirm that the list endpoint returns `quality`. If it doesn't, T3 changes to using the search endpoint, or it waits.
